SLIDE 1 · TITLE
  Title:    "Police Department · CCTV Video Intelligence"
  Subtitle: "Turn hours of footage into investigative answers in seconds"
  Footer:   IIS Technology  ·  Built on Red Hat OpenShift AI

SLIDE 2 · THE PROBLEM
  Three stats stacked, each with one-line caption:
    ▸ 90% of CCTV footage is never reviewed
    ▸ A 10-min clip takes 30+ min of operator attention to triage
    ▸ Operators are drowning while crime patterns repeat across clips
  Bottom line: "We are not making more cameras useful — we are making the
  ones we have searchable."
  Speaker note: relatable pain — 24/7 cameras, finite officers, missed
  patterns, manual logbooks.

SLIDE 3 · THE SOLUTION (one paragraph, no jargon)
  "Operator drops a CCTV clip. Within ~3 minutes the platform extracts
   what's visible (vehicle make/model, persons, faces, license plates),
   transcribes any audio, indexes everything in a searchable knowledge
   base, and lets investigators ask plain-English questions across one
   clip or thousands. Every answer comes with clickable timestamps that
   jump straight to the moment in the video, and a chain-of-custody log
   that satisfies evidence-handling requirements."
  Below: a single-line CTA — "All on-prem. All auditable. Frames never
  leave the cluster."

SLIDE 7 · USE CASE 1 — Smash-and-grab investigation
  Mini case-study layout:
    • Scenario: Storefront break-in caught on parking-lot CCTV
    • Without the platform: 4–6 hours of operator time to identify vehicle,
      crew size, sequence of events, and salvageable evidence
    • With the platform: 3 minutes ingest, then plain-English Q&A:
        "What vehicle? Make, model, plate?"
        "How many suspects? What were they wearing?"
        "What evidence did they leave?"
    • Outcome: Detective walks into the briefing with a typed timeline
      and clickable timestamps, not a stack of footage.

SLIDE 8 · USE CASE 2 — Missing-person canvas
  • Scenario: Eight cameras around a transit hub, eight hours of footage
  • Persona search: "Show me anyone matching this description in
    the last 4 hours" — semantic search across all clips at once
  • Output: Ranked list of clips with face/clothing matches and seekable
    timestamps; investigator confirms which leads to pursue
  • Time-to-lead: hours → minutes

SLIDE 9 · USE CASE 3 — Pattern-of-life / repeat offender
  • Scenario: Same suspect believed to be casing multiple locations
  • Index every clip from a week of patrols → query "loitering near
    storefront after hours"
  • Output: Cross-clip cluster with timestamps, confidence scores,
    chain-of-custody trail for every read

SLIDE 10 · USE CASE 4 — Public-records request / press response
  • Scenario: News organization requests a narrative of a public incident
  • Journalist persona produces a plain-English account from indexed data
    with timestamps for verification
  • Cuts response time from days (manual review by PIO) to hours

SLIDE 11 · BENEFITS (three columns)
  Operational
    ▸ ~10× faster triage — minutes per clip vs. hours
    ▸ Plain-English search across full archive
    ▸ Clickable timestamps eliminate scrubbing
  Compliance / Trust

    ▸ Human-in-the-loop required before any answer is "final"
    ▸ Frames never leave the cluster — fully on-prem inference
  Cost / Operations
    ▸ Runs on the OpenShift you already own
    ▸ GPU on-demand, scales to zero between clips
    ▸ No per-camera licence fees
